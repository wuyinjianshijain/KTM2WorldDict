# Deep design framework

Read this reference only after the user explicitly requests comprehensive design, a four-part structure, or deep expansion. Adapt the sections to the artifact; do not pad them to equal length.

## 1. Core positioning

- Define the design's central tension and why it deserves to exist in this world.
- State the player-facing promise and its role in the party's conflict or growth.
- Extract a small set of visual, audio, motion, material, or performance keywords for production teams.

## 2. World logic and execution

- Explain the relevant ecology, geography, institutions, incentives, mechanisms, or social relationships.
- Show the ordinary-person layer: livelihood, price, ritual, fear, taboo, slang, work, and adaptation.
- Identify who benefits, who pays, what evidence remains, and what information is restricted.
- Define dependencies on existing canon and distinguish confirmed facts from proposals.

## 3. Gameplay and production hooks

Select only applicable hooks:

- quest trigger, escalation, decision, consequence, and reveal;
- map rhythm, traversal rule, landmark, environmental interaction, and obstacle;
- combat identity, enemy behavior, status interaction, drop, counterplay, and party synergy;
- progression gate, reward cadence, economy link, or crafting dependency;
- art, animation, VFX, audio, UI, dialogue, and localization requirements.

Hooks must implement the setting rather than merely decorate it.

If the expansion includes a speaking character, opening quote, relationship sample, or voiced scene, run the `Character voice and quote gate` in `SKILL.md`. A comprehensive design is incomplete when character dialogue exists only as theme summaries or a single calm voice state.

## 4. Risks and alternatives

- Identify canon conflicts, scope growth, content burden, technical risk, readability problems, and likely repetition.
- Keep the recommended option in the main design.
- Offer two or three alternatives only for unresolved, consequential decisions.
- For each alternative, state creative benefit, systemic cost, production cost, and downstream impact.
