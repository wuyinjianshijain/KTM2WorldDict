# Character voice and dialogue standard

Use this whenever the work includes a character card, opening quote, sample dialogue, relationship line, voiced quest, party banter, or dialogue audit.

## 1. Before drafting

Answer all three. A blank in any of them means you are not ready to write the line.

**Scene card**

| Field | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Addressee | Who hears this, and what is their current relationship? |
| Place and action | Where are they, and what is the speaker physically doing? |
| Immediate goal | What must the listener do, understand, stop, or answer now? |
| Surface emotion | What can the listener hear immediately? |
| Concealed emotion | What leaks through despite the speaker's intention? |
| Listener response | What plausible answer, action, or interruption follows? |

**Project tone.** Read the project's authoring guide before choosing a register. A bright royal-road adventure and a grim political drama reject different lines. Never infer the register from the darkest fact in the lore: dark backstory does not license a dark voice, and in most JRPG projects the public-facing register is warmer than the design documents behind it.

**Canon constraints.** Verify every physical or biographical claim the line makes against the character's own entry: where they can be, what they can reach, how they arrived, whom they may address. A line that quietly asserts an impossible fact is a canon bug, not a style preference.

## 2. Voice fingerprint

Define a reusable fingerprint instead of a list of adjectives:

- default sentence length and rhythm;
- vocabulary learned from profession, region, class, and daily life;
- what the character notices first in a room;
- how they ask for help, and how they avoid asking;
- what happens to syntax when angry, frightened, embarrassed, exhausted, or delighted;
- humor source: observation, exaggeration, dryness, teasing, self-protection, or none;
- repair behavior after hurting someone.

Catchphrases, honorifics, dialect spelling, and verbal tics are not a fingerprint.

## 3. Required state coverage

| State | What to reveal |
| --- | --- |
| Initial contact | Boundary, first impression, immediate practical goal |
| Ordinary life | Taste, routine, humor, mild annoyance, curiosity |
| Pressure / anger | What control falls away; rhythm must change |
| Cooperation | Trust shown through action rather than praise |
| Vulnerability | A concrete memory, bodily reaction, mistake, or request |
| Deep relationship | What access is newly allowed, without erasing duties |

These are performance states, not romance stages. Omit states that do not fit the character's screen role.

## 4. Opening quotes

An opening quote sits under a portrait and is often the first thing anyone learns about this character. It must work for a reader who has read nothing else.

**Two failure modes. Both are disqualifying.**

| Failure | Looks like | Caught by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Author thesis | A polished aphorism about fate, truth, or sacrifice; `不是 X，而是 Y`; a line summarizing the character's whole arc | Theme-removal test |
| Opaque fragment | An in-scene utterance that is technically grounded but unreadable cold: job jargon, a bare inventory or status line, an oblique reference only the lore explains | **Stranger test** |

Correcting one by overshooting into the other is the most common repeat failure of this skill. The target sits between them: a line spoken in a real moment that a stranger still understands at once.

Accept only when all of these hold:

- a stranger who has read nothing can say roughly who this person is;
- the listener and situation can be inferred from the line itself;
- another character could respond to it;
- personality shows through priority, timing, or word choice;
- it does not explain the character's entire arc.

Worked examples:

- Reject — 「誓言并非束缚，而是在背叛最方便时仍能指引人的光。」 Author thesis wearing quotation marks.
- Reject — 「热井三号今天只出四成水。军工炉减半，儿童配给不动。」 Grounded, in-voice, in-world, and still unreadable cold. Nothing tells a new reader who is speaking or why to care.
- Accept — 「请退后。……不，我没打算一个人逞强。等我数到三，你从左边上——我们一起。」 Training, formality, self-correction, and willingness to cooperate, all legible without context.

When a project separates public from internal character material, the opening quote and every public line must also clear that project's per-character spoiler list.

## 5. Chinese JRPG and light-novel readability

- Natural Simplified Chinese word order; do not carry Japanese or English syntax across mechanically.
- Let spoken fragments be fragments, but never at the cost of the stranger test.
- One main thought per line; split exposition into exchange beats.
- Concrete nouns from the scene: wet cloak, medicine bucket, cracked cup, door latch.
- Pauses only when the speaker changes course, suppresses a response, or needs breath.
- One precise image beats several ornate abstractions.
- Not every intimate line is a confession, every angry line a speech, or every joke a trope.
- Avoid wordplay that collapses outside Chinese unless the scene depends on it and localization notes are supplied.

## 6. Audit

Run in order. Do not approve while a hard failure stands.

1. **Stranger test** — hide all context. Can a first-time reader say who this is? Applies to opening quotes and any public-facing line.
2. **Name-removal test** — hide the speaker. Can a reader still narrow it to this character?
3. **Swap test** — could another cast member say it unchanged?
4. **Response test** — can the listener answer or act naturally?
5. **Theme-removal test** — delete the abstract moral. Does a human moment remain?
6. **Breath test** — speakable in that emotional state without stacked clauses?
7. **Canon test** — does every physical or biographical claim match the character's entry?
8. **Cross-cast isomorphism** — below. This is the test most often skipped.

Record deliberate exceptions only when a ritual speech, courtroom statement, prophecy, or public address genuinely requires formal rhetoric.

### Cross-cast isomorphism

Per-line tests cannot catch this, and a cast can fail it while every individual line passes. Lay every character's lines for the same state side by side and compare **skeletons**, not vocabulary: opening move, clause order, rhetorical template, punctuation rhythm, emotional landing.

Nine characters who each open by stopping the listener, correcting a misunderstanding, then inviting cooperation share one voice no matter how different their word choice.

Maintain a quota table in the project's authoring guide. Each template gets at most one owner across the core cast, named explicitly:

| Template | Allowed uses |
| --- | --- |
| Opening prohibition (别／退／趴下／把手给我) | 1 |
| `不是 X，是 Y` clarification | 1 |
| Em-dash reversal in the same slot | 1 |
| Knocking as the intimacy beat | 1 |
| "Sit with me" as the vulnerability beat | 1 |
| "Stop calling me X, call me Y" | 1 |

Location, faction, and item epigraphs need the same pass. Eight epigraphs built as "outsiders assume X, but locals know Y" are one narrator speaking eight times, not eight places. Vary the artifact itself: overheard dialogue, a posted regulation, graffiti with the institution's response, a meeting record, a duty log, a parent's instruction to a child.
